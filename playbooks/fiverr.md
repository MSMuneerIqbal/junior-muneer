# Fiverr playbook

On Fiverr, buyers come to {{user_name}}: through gigs, custom offers and the inbox. Fast, clear replies matter (response time shows on the profile), and Fiverr's system reads messages and flags anything that leads off Fiverr. Everything here is copy and paste: {{user_name}} sends it themself. (2,500 characters is a safe size for one message; most should be much shorter.)

## Structure
**Replies to buyers:**
- Thank them briefly and answer their question first.
- Confirm what they need in 1–3 short points, and ask what's missing (files, deadline, examples).
- Next step: a **custom offer** (scope, delivery time, revisions) or a short question. Keep everything inside Fiverr.

**Delivery messages:** what's delivered, how to use it, what's included (files, revisions left), and an invitation to ask for changes within the revisions. Friendly, never pushy about reviews.

**Revision replies:** acknowledge, restate the change, give a realistic time, and keep it within the agreed scope (anything bigger becomes a new offer).

## Tone
- Friendly, quick and professional. Short sentences; easy to read on a phone.
- Specific to the buyer's request; no generic templates.

## Hashtags
- None.

## Red lines
- **Never share an email address, phone number, WhatsApp/Telegram/Skype or other app, or social media handles; never send links that lead off Fiverr.** Share work through Fiverr's own attachments and delivery. (Fiverr's system flags these, and accounts get warnings or bans.)
- **No payment outside Fiverr** (no PayPal, bank transfer or crypto), and never ask for one.
- **Never ask for or offer anything in exchange for a review** (no "5 stars for a discount").
- Never claim experience, numbers or results that aren't in the profile; never promise what the gig doesn't include.
