# Upwork playbook

Upwork is {{user_name}}'s income. Proposals and client messages are read by busy clients who skim, and Upwork bans accounts that break its rules. Everything here is copy and paste: {{user_name}} submits and sends it themself.

## Structure
**Proposals** (cover letters; up to 5,000 characters, but 150–250 words win):
- **The first 2–3 lines are all the client sees in the list.** Open with their problem and how you'd solve it, in their words. Never "Dear Sir/Madam", "I am writing to apply" or your name first.
- **Proof:** one or two real, relevant projects or results from the profile ("I built a hospital assistant with 47 database tools over MCP"), with a portfolio or GitHub link if it helps.
- **A short plan:** 2–4 steps or a first milestone, so the client sees how you'd work.
- **One smart question** about their project, which shows you read it and starts a conversation.
- **Close** simply: availability and a friendly line. No begging, no "please give me a chance".
- Answer every screening question the job asks, in order.

**Client messages:** answer what they asked first, in short paragraphs; then the next step (a question, a call through Upwork, a milestone). Polite, warm and businesslike.

## Tone
- Simple, clear English; confident but honest. Write like a capable person talking to a client, not a template.
- Specific beats general: name their tools, their goal, their deadline.
- No hype or AI-sounding phrases; no exaggeration.

## Hashtags
- None. Hashtags don't belong in proposals or messages.

## Red lines
- **No contact or payment outside Upwork before a contract:** no email address, phone number, WhatsApp, Telegram, Skype or other apps; no PayPal, bank transfer, crypto or "pay me directly". (Upwork bans accounts for this.)
- If a client asks to move off Upwork or pay outside it, politely keep it on Upwork, and treat it as a warning sign.
- Never claim experience, clients, numbers or results that aren't in the profile. Never promise guaranteed results.
- No copy-paste generic proposals: every proposal answers this job.
- No rates or prices unless the job asks for them or a rate is set in the profile.

## Judging a job (for the Freelance Manager)
- **Good signs:** payment verified, a hire rate above about 50%, money spent before, good reviews, a clear description and budget, skills that match the profile.
- **Warning signs:** no payment method, a brand-new client with an unclear job, a budget far below the work, many proposals already.
- **Scam signs (skip and say why):** asks to contact them on WhatsApp/Telegram/email, asks you to pay or buy something first, asks for personal or bank details, "send a free sample of the full work", a job that's only a link to click, or a check/overpayment story.
